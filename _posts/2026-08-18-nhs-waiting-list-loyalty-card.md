title: "NHS Trust Launches Waiting List 'Loyalty Card', Tenth Appointment Free"
standfirst: "Patients who collect enough stamps before the card expires will be entitled to a free follow-up appointment, subject to a separate waiting list."
category: Economy
author: Our Health Correspondent

An NHS trust has begun issuing patients on its outpatient waiting list with a laminated loyalty card, sponsored by the soft drinks brand Fizzade, awarding one stamp for every missed target. Five stamps earns a family-sized share bag of Fizzade crisps; ten unlocks a free follow-up consultation, plus a two-litre bottle of full-sugar Fizzade "as a thank you for sticking with us." A trust spokesperson described the sponsorship as "a sensible way to fund the scheme at no cost to the taxpayer," while confirming that the card itself does not affect anyone's position on the actual waiting list.

Gerald, of Berkshire, said he had written to his MP after his card expired one stamp short of the free appointment, having waited eleven months for what he understood to be a routine cardiology referral. "I'm not against a loyalty scheme in principle," he wrote. "I collect stamps at the car wash. I wasn't expecting two litres of cola for my heart." He said the crisps had been "very nice" but he wasn't sure they were what the consultant had in mind.

A health campaign group noted that the scheme's most frequent card-holders were disproportionately drawn from the trust's own cardiology and diabetes clinics, a pattern the trust described as "coincidental" and "not something we currently track." The trust confirmed a renewal of the sponsorship deal was already under discussion, citing "strong demand" and a further product range expected in the new year.

A new analysis by the Albion Policy Foundation, Tufton Street, found the average patient now earns enough stamps for two free appointments and several kilograms of confectionery before receiving their first real one, a figure the Foundation called "stark," while adding it had not been able to determine whether any patient had actually redeemed either.

The trust said it remained committed to reducing waiting times and confirmed the free tenth appointment was "extremely popular," with current bookings running approximately six months out.
