Visitors to National Trust properties face a culinary overhaul this autumn after the conservation charity announced it will rename traditional fish and chips across all 300 of its tearooms and cafes to ensure its menu remains "radically inclusive and non-triggering."

Under new internal guidance quietly issued to catering staff this week, the classic seaside staple will henceforth be listed on menus as "Pan-Fried Sub-Aquatic Protein accompanied by Thermally Crisp Potato Wedges."

The move comes following what insiders describe as a six-month "linguistic impact audit" conducted by an independent cultural consultancy.

According to leaked briefing notes, the word "fish" was flagged for potentially triggering visitors who practise veganism or suffer from severe ichthyophobia (the fear of fish). Meanwhile, the term "chips" was deemed problematic due to its historical association with "class-based fast-food stereotyping and oppressive frying culture."

A spokesperson for the National Trust defended the decision, insisting the institution must adapt to modern sensibilities.

"Our heritage sites welcome millions of diverse visitors each year. By shifting toward descriptive, neutral nomenclature, we remove the emotional baggage tied to historic food terminology while celebrating sustainable plant- and marine-sourced nutrients."

The guidance further specifies that terms like "battered" must be strictly avoided on printed menus to prevent distress, recommending instead that staff refer to the golden crispy exterior as a "textural wheat-based encapsulation layer." Tartare sauce will also undergo a nomenclature refresh, re-emerging as "Emulsified Herb and Caper Dressing."

The rebrand has already drawn sharp criticism from heritage campaigners and regular visitors.

"It is absolute madness," said Sir Reginald Thorne, founder of the Save Our Chippy campaign group. "You go to a National Trust property to look at an Elizabethan manor and enjoy a piece of battered cod, not to decode a chemistry textbook. What's next? Renaming a cream tea a 'bovine emulsion spread on leavened dough'?"

Despite the backlash, trust officials confirmed the menu roll-out will begin at flagship properties next month, accompanied by single-origin salt crystals and a newly created "Terminology Support Desk," staffed by volunteers trained to explain the new names to bemused visitors and issue laminated glossary cards translating each dish back into English on request.

When asked if traditional Cornish pasties or Victoria sponges were next on the chopping block, a Trust spokesperson declined to comment, though rumours suggest pasties are currently being evaluated under the working title "Crimp-Folded Root Vegetable Envelopes."
