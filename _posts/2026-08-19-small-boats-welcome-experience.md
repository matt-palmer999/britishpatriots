The Home Office has confirmed that small boat arrivals processed at reception centres will now be issued a complimentary umbrella as part of a new "Welcome Experience," intended, according to the department, "to manage expectations about British weather." A spokesperson said the initiative reflected the department's wider commitment to "getting the basics right."

The accompanying orientation card includes a short briefing on British weather patterns, distinguishing between "light rain," "persistent rain" and "the sort that gets in your shoes regardless of umbrella," alongside guidance on the term "bright spells," which the card notes should not be taken as a promise. Officials said the briefing had tested well in focus groups, held over two days in a conference room in Croydon.

A breakdown released alongside the announcement showed the umbrella scheme costs £34 per head once branding, distribution and storage are factored in, marginally more than the department's own estimated cost of processing a single asylum claim. Asked about the discrepancy, the spokesperson called it "not a like-for-like comparison."

The Welcome Experience will also include a laminated orientation card and a short satisfaction survey, modelled, officials said, on similar schemes used by "leading UK visitor attractions." Early results are not yet available, though the department confirmed the survey does not currently ask about processing times, decision backlogs, or accommodation.

Gerald, of Berkshire, said he had written to his MP to ask why nobody had offered him a laminated welcome card or a satisfaction survey the last time he queued for two hours at the passport office, and had been told the schemes were "not comparable." He said he intended to write again.

The average wait for an initial asylum decision remains unchanged. The umbrella, a department source confirmed, arrives within 48 hours, and is expected, in keeping with tradition, to invert in the wind within the first week.
