Facial recognition cameras newly installed along the high street as part of the council's town centre safety scheme flagged a "high-confidence match" to Terry Ogden, 54, a shoplifter well known to local officers for his distinctive shaved head, after locking onto a bald mannequin positioned in a menswear shop window.

Police, alerted automatically by the council's system, monitored the feed for the following six hours, logging the "suspect" as showing "no signs of leaving" and "displaying unusually rigid posture consistent with evasion training." A plain clothes officer, tasked with walking past, noted that the suspect appeared to be naked apart from an expensive cashmere jumper and some y-fronts. The officer also reported back that the suspect had "demonstrated superhuman eye-contact discipline whilst acting suspiciously calm for a man frozen in mid-stride with his trousers missing."

A council spokesperson said the camera scheme, installed at a cost of £280,000, had "already demonstrated the system is live, responsive, and extremely thorough," and confirmed a formal commendation for the officers involved was "still being considered." A police spokesperson, asked separately about the six-hour observation, said officers were "trained to observe, not speculate," when asked whether anyone monitoring the feed had considered that the subject never blinked.

Meanwhile, two doors down, the real Terry Ogden was captured on standard-definition CCTV casually strolling out of a newsagent with three packets of Fox's Chocolate Digestives.

Ogden, approached regarding the newsagent incident, pointed out that police were welcome to check their own six-hour surveillance log, which placed him "standing very still in a jumper" at the exact time the biscuits went missing, and said he considered the matter "closed."
